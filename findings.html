1. Seventh chords go from exception to default

Share of chord events with a seventh or higher extension, mean over pieces, with 95% bootstrap bands. Classical periods are 25-year bins of composition date; jazz and rock/pop are decades.

Chords with a seventh or extension

ClassicalJazzRock & pop

Ninths, elevenths, thirteenths

Under 2% before 1800; 18% in early-20th-century classical music; 12% in post-bop.

Goudimel's 1565 psalms contain no sevenths; Corelli (1680s) uses them in 22% of chords; Bach to Beethoven sit at 25–34%; Chopin, Grieg and Tchaikovsky pass 45%; Bartók 69%, Schulhoff 96%. Jazz starts where late Romanticism ends (63% in the swing era) and reaches 91% by bebop. Rock and pop stay at 21–29% in every decade: a 1985 single is closer to a Mozart sonata than to a 1985 jazz solo.

2. The dominant loses its grip

Of all moves onto a tonic chord, the share coming from V, from IV and from bVII. The authentic cadence is a plateau, not a constant: about half of tonic arrivals in the Renaissance, 62–67% from 1700 to 1850, 43% after 1900. Rock repeats the decline in thirty years.

3. Chromaticism is flat for two centuries, then climbs

A chord is non-diatonic when its root lies outside the local key or its quality is not the one the key supplies. The share is 20–24% from Corelli to Schubert, 29% after 1850, 40% after 1900. Jazz inherits the late-Romantic level and extends it; rock is the most diatonic music in the corpus.

4. Richness and predictability come apart

Vocabulary barely changes for three centuries while transition entropy, how unpredictable the next chord is given the current one, rises from 2.7 bits in the Renaissance to 4.5 bits after 1900. Jazz is the extreme case: the most sevenths and the lowest entropy (2.3–2.6 bits), because ii7–V7–I7 repeats through every key.

5. Three grammars

The most common chords and progressions in each era show the grammar directly. Roman numerals are relative to the local key; 7 marks any seventh chord, 9 any higher extension.

Top chords and top progressions by era

EraTop chords (share of events)Top progressions (share of moves)
  1. Classical music is the most concentrated: V7→I alone is 10% of all progressions in 1750–99, twice its share in any other era.
  2. Jazz is sevenths and the circle of fifths: every top chord is a seventh chord and ii7→V7→I7 supplies the two commonest moves.
  3. Rock is I, IV and V as neighbours: I→IV and IV→I lead every period, V→IV is in the top six after 1965, bVII is a top-six chord from the late 1960s.

Does harmony follow time or place?

Mantel tests on pairwise distances between composers and artists: correlation of harmonic distance with distance in years and with distance in kilometres between base cities, and partial versions that remove the other factor. p-values from 5,000 permutations.

SubsetUnitsr (time)pr (geography)pr (geo | time)pr (time | geo)p

Within classical music, jazz and rock alike, composers close in time are close in harmony. Within classical music and jazz, composers close in space are not, once time is accounted for. Rock and pop show only a weak, non-significant geographic signal (r = 0.10): Memphis, Nashville and Macon against London and Los Angeles. Pooled across genres, geography looks significant and time does not, but that is the Atlantic standing in for genre and a history in which the 1560s and the 1960s sound more alike than either does to 1800. Inside the classical corpus, a piece's harmonically nearest neighbour by another composer is a median 48 years away (random pairs: 84) and in the same city only 10% of the time (random: 14%).

Method and sources

  • Corpora. DCML Distant Listening Corpus and When in Rome (expert Roman-numeral analyses, 2,058 classical pieces, 1565–1931); Rock Corpus RS200 (Roman numerals, 193 songs); McGill Billboard (chord symbols with tonic, 876 singles, 1958–91); Weimar Jazz Database (lead-sheet changes for 412 solos, 1925–2007). Modern corpora via ChoCo.
  • Features. Every chord label becomes: root as scale degree above the local tonic; quality; extension (triad, seventh, ninth and above); non-diatonic flag; applied-chord flag and inversion where annotated; duration in beats. Consecutive identical chords are merged.
  • Metrics are computed per piece, then averaged over pieces; intervals are 400 bootstrap resamples of pieces. Vocabulary is rarefied to 40 chords; transition entropy is H(next | current) pooled per era.
  • Space-time. Points at (longitude, latitude, year), one century scaled to 1,000 km, Ward clustering into scenes; composers clustered on z-scored mean profiles (Ward); Mantel and partial Mantel tests with 5,000 permutations.
  • Caveats. Classical and Rock Corpus labels are expert Roman numerals; Billboard and Weimar are chord symbols, so cross-genre levels are partly notation. Sampling is uneven (1700–49 is largely Bach chorales; 1900–49 is small). Locations are residences, not composition sites.
  • Sources. DCML · When in Rome · ChoCo · Rock Corpus · Weimar Jazz Database. Related work: White 2013/2017; Weiß et al. 2018; Moss et al. 2022; Harasim et al. 2021; González-Espinoza & Plotkin 2023; Park et al. 2020; Broze & Shanahan 2013; Mauch et al. 2015; Gauvin 2015; de Clercq & Temperley 2011; Schich et al. 2014; Georges & Nguyen 2019.
